## 1. Purpose and position

**What Fintiq stands for:** fundamental analysis and long-term investing, not speculative trading. The channel, the website and the agents all follow that.

**What we are building:**
1. A YouTube channel that analyses the Magnificent 7 as businesses: Apple, Alphabet, Nvidia, Meta, Microsoft, Tesla, Amazon.
2. A Mag 7 data hub on fintiq.uk, built from free official sources plus daily closing prices entered by hand.
3. Two AI agents: one collects and presents the data; the other analyses it and drafts content, with an interpretation feature (section 5).
4. Expansion beyond the seven once there is revenue.

**Our position (from the 12 Nov research):**
- Almost all existing Mag 7 content is US-made, trading-focused content: chart levels, price targets, "buy or sell".
- Very few creators hold financial qualifications, and studies show creators' stock picks have done worse than simply holding an index fund.
- Our gap: calm, qualified, plain-English analysis of the *business*. We judge each day's price move against the fundamentals, and speak to UK investors in pounds (including the many who own these companies through pensions and tracker funds without knowing it).

**Our promise:** "Clearer and more useful than Bloomberg for these seven companies, for a long-term investor." We don't try to beat Bloomberg on speed or breadth; we beat it on depth, clarity and judging news against the fundamentals.

**Never:** buy/sell/hold calls, price targets, chart-pattern trading, hype titles.

## 3. Content line-up

### Format A: Quarterly results analysis (one thorough video per company per quarter)
**When:** within 24–48 hours of each company's results. 28 videos a year.

**Fixed template:**
1. **Headline numbers vs last year and vs the company's own guidance:** revenue, operating profit, EPS, free cash flow.
2. **Segments:** which parts of the business grew or shrank (e.g. Apple iPhone vs Services; Microsoft Cloud; Amazon AWS vs retail; Nvidia data centre).
3. **Margins and costs:** gross and operating margin trend, R&D, capital spending.
4. **Cash and balance sheet:** cash, debt, buybacks, dividends, share-based pay.
5. **Guidance and the earnings call:** what management said about next quarter and the key themes. Summarised in our own words from the company's webcast and slides.
6. **What changed in the long-term story:** the most important section.
7. **The market's reaction:** price move on the day, split into market, sector and company (Catalyst Atlas), and how the price behaved after past results.
8. **Valuation in context:** P/E, price-to-sales and free-cash-flow yield, compared with the company's own history. Framed as "what has to be true for today's price to make sense", never as a verdict.
9. **What to watch next quarter.**

**Built on:** Deep Dive (pre-filled pack the night before), EDGAR, the company's press release and slides, Catalyst Atlas.

### Format B: Daily bulletin, "Mag 7 Today" (8–10 minutes, every trading day)
**When:** after the US close. Recorded the same evening, or first thing in the morning for UK viewers.

**Running order:**
1. **Scoreboard (60 seconds):** all seven. Move today, and how much was the market, the sector and the company (Atlas).
2. **The one or two that matter:** any real company-specific move or story, judged against the fundamentals: "Does this change the business, or not?"
3. **Business news:** products, regulation, lawsuits, management changes, deals.
4. **Earnings calls and events:** when they happen, the key points summarised.
5. **Fundamentals check:** where each price sits against its valuation history, updated with today's close.
6. **Coming up:** results dates, product events, economic data that affects these seven.

**Editorial line:** most days the honest message is "the market moved, the business didn't". Saying so plainly is the channel's point of view.

**Spin-offs:** 1–2 Shorts (60 seconds) cut from each bulletin; a written version as the Fintiq daily email.

### Format C: 10-K deep-dive series (about 6–7 episodes per company per year)
**When:** after each company files its annual report (10-K), staggered through the year (section 4).

**Episodes:**
1. **The business:** what it sells, to whom, how it makes money, competitive position (10-K Item 1).
2. **Income statement:** revenue, costs and profit over 5 years, by segment.
3. **Balance sheet:** assets, debt, cash, working capital, goodwill.
4. **Cash flow:** operating cash, capital spending, free cash flow, buybacks, share-based pay.
5. **Risks and legal matters:** risk factors (Item 1A) and lawsuits (Item 3), in plain English.
6. **Board, pay and governance:** from the proxy statement (DEF 14A): directors, executive pay, voting control, shareholder proposals.
7. **Putting it together:** management's own discussion (MD&A), the shareholder letter where one exists (e.g. Amazon), and how the business has changed over the year. Not a price target.

**Note:** US annual reports don't have a UK-style chairman's statement. The governance and pay material is in the separate proxy statement, and some companies publish a shareholder letter.

### Supporting formats (as time allows)
- **"In pounds":** what the Mag 7 returned to a UK investor after the pound/dollar move. Monthly.
- **"Your pension and the Mag 7":** how much of a typical tracker or pension fund sits in these seven. Quarterly.
- **Evergreen explainers:** how to read a 10-K, what P/E means, W-8BEN, how results days work.

## 5. Interpretation feature (Imran to specify at the agent stage)

**Purpose:** the Analyst agent doesn't just report numbers; it explains what they mean for the business, in Fintiq's fundamentals-first voice, with every claim traceable to the data.

**Initial ideas to discuss (not decided):**
- **Read-across:** "Revenue grew 8% but receivables grew 25%; customers are paying more slowly" (an accountant's reading of the statements).
- **Quality of earnings:** profit vs cash flow, one-off items, share-based pay, capitalised costs.
- **Change detection:** what's new in this 10-K vs last year's (risk factors added or removed, segment changes, new lawsuits, wording changes in MD&A).
- **News vs fundamentals:** for each daily move, does the news touch revenue, margins, cash or risk, or is it sentiment?
- **Valuation context:** what growth rate today's price implies, and how that compares with the company's history. Framed as context, never a verdict.
- **Confidence labels:** every interpretation marked as "from the filing", "management's claim" or "our reading", so viewers see what is fact and what is judgement.

**Imran's inputs to collect at that stage:** the interpretation rules and checklists he uses as an accountant/analyst; the house style for verdict-free conclusions; examples of good and bad interpretations.

### 6a. Free and publishable
| What | Source | How often |
|---|---|---|
| Financial statements, segments (10+ years) | SEC EDGAR XBRL APIs | After each 10-Q / 10-K |
| 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K text (risk factors, MD&A, legal) | SEC EDGAR filings | As filed |
| Proxy statements (board, pay, governance) | SEC EDGAR DEF 14A | Yearly |
| Insider buying and selling | SEC Form 4 | Daily |
| Results dates, press releases, slides, webcast links | Company IR sites | Daily check |
| Earnings calls | Company webcast / replay. Summarise in our own words; don't reproduce paid transcripts | Each quarter |
| US interest rates | US Treasury / Fed H.15 | Daily |
| Oil | US EIA (credit with date) | Daily |
| GBP/USD | Bank of England daily spot rate (check reuse terms; credit) | Daily |
| US/UK economy | BLS, BEA, ONS, Bank of England | On release |
| Tracker-fund holdings | Public fund factsheets (credit the provider) | Monthly |

### 6b. Daily closing prices: entered by hand
- **What:** today's close for the 7 stocks and the chosen ETFs (SPY, QQQ, sector ETFs). About 15–17 numbers.
- **When:** each US trading day, around 21:30 UK time.
- **How:** typed into a simple password-protected form on Fintiq (or a sheet the agent reads), with the source checked for each.
- **Rules:**
  - check each close in two places;
  - build history day by day from the start date;
  - **don't hand-copy back-history** from Yahoo or similar (the UK database right covers copying a large part of a database by any method);
  - long-term pictures use EDGAR fundamentals until FMP arrives.
- **Agent checks before accepting:**
  - all values present (or a market holiday);
  - any move over 15% flagged unless a filing or news explains it;
  - the date is a real trading day;
  - share splits flagged.

- At the point of revenue: FMP commercial plan (quoted $600/month) for full price history, wider coverage and expansion beyond the seven.

### 6d. Not available (accept the gap)

## 8. The two agents

### Agent 1: Collector and Presenter
- **Job:** fetch, check, store and present. Mostly scheduled code; AI only for summarising news and extracting sections from filings.
- **Daily jobs:**
  - EDGAR filings every 2 hours;
  - news sweeps 3 times a day;
  - Imran's manual closes at ~21:30 UK, then checks;
  - the Atlas split for all seven;
  - valuation ratios;
  - the hub page update;
  - the daily package handed to Agent 2.
- **Results days:** the night before, pre-fill the Deep Dive pack. When the results come out, load the new figures and the press release.
- **Presents:**
  - `mag7.html` on Fintiq: a daily line per company, a company card (price vs fundamentals, last 4 quarters, next results, filings, insiders, news), a comparison chart;
  - `/mag7/...` endpoints for Deep Dive and Catalyst Atlas.

### Agent 2: Analyst and Writer
- **Job:** interpret (section 5) and draft content (section 3). Uses only hub facts; every number points to a hub row.
- **Drafts:**
  - daily bulletin script + Shorts + email (by ~22:30 UK);
  - results video scripts (within 24 hours of results);
  - 10-K episode scripts (from the extracted filing sections);
  - a chart list for each.
- **Writing rules:**
  - plain English;
  - explain every term the first time it appears;
  - always split a move into market / sector / company;
  - judge every news story against revenue, margins, cash or risk;
  - every opinion labelled as "our reading";
  - every script ends: "This is education, not advice. Fintiq does not tell you what to buy or sell."
- **Banned:** buy, sell, hold, "price target", "this stock will…", "guaranteed", "can't lose", "undervalued/overvalued" as a verdict.
- **Review:** drafts only. Imran edits and approves. Nothing is uploaded automatically.

## 10. Compliance checklist
- **FCA financial promotions:**
  - read the finfluencer guidance before launch;
  - education and analysis only;
  - no verdicts, targets or calls on named shares;
  - get advice before any sponsorship or affiliate deal.
- **Adviser route:** tell the IFA network about the channel when joining; keep it strictly non-personal and educational.
- **Data:**
  - closes from public sources, entered daily, no bulk copying;
  - FMP only under a written commercial licence;
  - credit EIA, BoE, ONS and fund providers;
  - SEC User-Agent and rate limits.
- **Words:** news and calls summarised in our own words with links; no copying articles or paid transcripts.
- **YouTube:** Imran on camera in his own voice; disclose any AI-generated visuals; avoid template-identical uploads.
- **Corrections:** errors fixed in the description and a pinned comment the same day.
