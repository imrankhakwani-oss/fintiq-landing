Methodology & Disclaimer

Methodology

This report uses Monte Carlo simulation to model 5,000 independent retirement scenarios. Each simulation draws annual investment returns from a log-normal distribution parameterised by your chosen expected return and volatility. This reflects the real-world reality that returns are not constant year-to-year — some years markets rise sharply, others fall. By running thousands of scenarios, we estimate the realistic distribution of outcomes rather than a single deterministic forecast.

The model incorporates three phases: (1) Accumulation — savings grow with market returns and contributions until retirement; (2) Bridge — the period between retirement and State Pension start age, during which full income must be drawn from the pot; (3) Decumulation — withdrawals continue, reduced by State Pension income, until life expectancy. All monetary values are expressed in today's money (real terms) using your specified inflation rate.

A simulation is considered a "failure" if the pot reaches zero before the specified life expectancy. The success rate is the percentage of simulations that do not fail.

Important Limitations

This model does not account for: tax on pension withdrawals, means-tested benefits, care costs in later life, early death, changes in State Pension legislation, or currency risk. Returns are modelled as normally distributed, which may underestimate tail risks (extreme crashes). The model assumes contributions are consistent — irregular contributions will produce different results.
