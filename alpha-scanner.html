Important: Alpha Scanner is a research screen of US-listed small companies (about $50m to $2bn market value), excluding banks, insurers and other financials. These are small, often illiquid companies. Each signal lists the rules a company passed, taken from its latest quarterly or annual SEC filings; it is not investment advice or a personal recommendation. Past performance is not indicative of future results. Always conduct your own research and consider speaking to a qualified financial adviser before investing.

How a company gets here
All five must hold, using the company's latest 12 months of figures (its last four quarterly or annual filings): (1) non-financial US company, revenue of $50m or more, at least 3 years of history, market value $50m–$2bn; (2) still struggling: a loss in the latest 12 months or the 12 months before, or an operating margin under 3%; (3) shares at or below half of their 3-year high; (4) S1 cash beats earnings (operating cash flow positive despite a loss) or S3 shrink to grow (revenue down, gross profit up); (5) not in the Altman Z bankruptcy-risk zone. Extra signals S2, S4, S5 and S6 are shown when present, and a Lower risk label marks companies that also pass the quality check (strong quality score, positive cash flow, no sign of earnings manipulation). Every new filing is checked again. If the latest figures no longer pass, the company moves to No longer qualifies with the reason (figures got worse, price recovered, back to profit, and so on). Its returns are still counted in the track record. In a test on 2018–2023 filings (258 cases), companies passing these rules had a median 3-year return of +41%; 33% doubled and 19% halved. That test used annual filings; using the latest quarters lets a turnaround show up sooner but has not been tested separately. Results varied a lot by year, and past results do not predict future returns.
