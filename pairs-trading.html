Cointegration & Mean Reversion

Two stocks can be cointegrated even if individually non-stationary — meaning their price ratio tends to revert to a long-run equilibrium. Classic examples include Coca-Cola/Pepsi (same market, same consumer), JPM/BAC (identical interest rate exposure), or XOM/CVX (oil price-driven). The key is shared economic drivers, not just historical correlation.

📏

Z-Score & Entry Signals

The spread Z-score = (current ratio − rolling mean) / rolling standard deviation. When Z > +2, Asset A is relatively expensive vs Asset B — the trade is: short A, long B. When Z < −2, the reverse. The position is closed when Z reverts toward 0. A ±2 threshold means the spread is beyond 2 standard deviations from its mean — historically a high-probability mean-reversion setup.

⚠️

Risk Factors to Watch

Pairs trades carry regime-change risk: the relationship can permanently break (e.g. one company changes its business model, gets acquired, or faces sector-specific regulatory shock). Stop losses are essential. Lookback period matters — too short creates noise, too long misses recent structural shifts.
